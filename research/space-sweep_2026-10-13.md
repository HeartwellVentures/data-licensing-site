# AI Training-Data Licensing Space: Research Sweep

**Date:** 2026-10-13 · **Method:** read-only web research (no outreach, no sign-ups)
**Purpose:** Inform Jay's partner-led data-licensing business and the website build. Model: companies LICENSE (never "sell") heavily anonymized operational data to frontier AI labs and retain ownership. Partners recruit data-owning companies, bring deals, split revenue.
**Research validity:** company facts ~6-12 months; prices/payouts ~60-90 days.

**Headline findings:**
- The space is real and heating up. Micro1 ($1B commitment, Oct 9, 2026), Troveo ($20M+ paid out), Wirestock ($15M paid), Defined.ai (10+ years, named lab customers) all validate that labs pay brokers for training-ready, rights-cleared data.
- Micro1's public partner program pays flat referral bounties (up to $50K), NOT revenue splits. Jay's split deal is bespoke; the agreement text must be verified.
- Pewter Data is the closest positioning comp but has a red flag: its paying "licensing partner" is anonymous, with no verifiable team, funding, or lab relationships.
- No competitor publishes partner commission splits. That is white space for Jay's partner-led model.

## 1. Micro1 Deep Dive

**Program:** https://www.micro1.ai/data-partnerships — Company Data Partnerships. Micro1 licenses companies' operational data (SOPs, knowledge bases, CRM data, project histories, QA processes, decision patterns, human feedback on AI outputs) and turns it into reinforcement-learning "training environments" where frontier AI agents learn real business workflows.

**What they offer companies:**
- Hero framing: "Your company already has valuable data to train frontier AI models" / "micro1 helps companies monetize those assets through data licensing opportunities."
- Ownership: "Companies retain ownership of their underlying data."
- Privacy: PII removed "when relevant"; restricted access to limited authorized team members; isolated pipelines; limited retention; companies review representative samples before use; deletion requests honored. Some datasets get "synthetic rewrites that preserve workflow structure while reducing direct ties to original records."
- Process: assessment → evaluation → discovery call (data + privacy requirements) → partnership agreement → data delivery.
- Qualification: operationally mature, 30+ employees, established documentation, English-language data; US prioritized, then UK/Canada. Industries: software/engineering, finance/accounting, customer support, sales/CRM, legal/contracts, operations/logistics.

**Partner economics (public):**
- Company-side payout tiers: **$100K+** (qualified enterprise), **$500K+** (large-scale/ongoing), **$1M+** (highly unique proprietary data).
- Referrer-side: flat bounties, **up to $50K per successful company referral** (was $25K at the June 2026 launch; $5M launch pool). Paid only after the company onboards and hits revenue milestones (workflow partnerships) or data acceptance (corpus partnerships). Bounty credited to a micro1 wallet.
- Separate $3K bounty for individual expert referrals. "Payouts are flat, fixed amounts, not percentages."
- **Critical discrepancy: there is no public revenue-share/split model.** If Jay's deal is a true split, it is bespoke and beyond the public program. Verify the signed agreement.

**Credibility:** Substantially real. Founded 2022 by Ali Ansari (25, solo, Berkeley); pivoted early 2025 from AI recruiting ("Zara" interviewer) to human/expert data. $3.3M pre-seed (2023, Dream Ventures); $35M Series A at $500M (Sept 2025, 01 Advisors, M12, Calacanis); reportedly >$100M at $4B (Sept 2026, Forbes, unconfirmed). Claimed customers: Microsoft, Amazon, 1X, unnamed frontier labs. Inc. 5000 #37 (2025). $1B 12-month data commitment announced Oct 9, 2026, financed by Citi and Hercules Capital. Reported >$500M annualized run rate (company-reported, unaudited).

**Red flags:** (1) split-vs-bounty mismatch; (2) "$200M+ generated for partners" is self-reported; (3) $1B is a 4-day-old spending commitment via lenders, not cash in hand; (4) $4B valuation unconfirmed; (5) Spirit Airlines bankruptcy data bid ($12.5M vs Google's $10M): privacy ombudsman asked for more vetting time, unions/lawmakers objected, micro1 proposed self-de-identification; hearing Oct 14, 2026; (6) zero named partner companies or verified partner payouts; (7) typos on official partnership pages ("assesment," "comapny") suggest they were stood up fast.

**Serious operators, in brief:**
- **Micro1** — deepest pocket in the lane; $1B commitment; the buyer Jay is signing with.
- **Troveo** (troveo.ai) — $20M+ paid to 7,000+ rights holders (Apr 2026); $4.5M seed from Seven Seven Six; founder Marty Pesis (ex-Vouch). Expanded 2026 into business/enterprise data, on a collision course with micro1.
- **Protege** (withprotege.ai) — $65M total (a16z led both rounds); founders incl. ex-Datavant CEO Travis May; claims work with most of the Magnificent Seven. Health/media heavy, less SMB overlap.
- **Defined.ai** — operating since 2015, $85M+ raised, names Google/Meta/Apple/Amazon/Microsoft as licensees; only player with public unit pricing ($1-2/image, $0.001/word, suppliers keep 20-30%).
- **Wirestock** — $23M Series A (May 2026, Nava Ventures, Sheryl Sandberg's SBVP); claims $40M run rate, $15M paid out, 700K contributors. Creator lane, not company data.

**Hype/unproven:** Pewter (anonymous payer), Kled AI, Vana (collapsed token), Versos AI, Verb, Ocean Protocol. None has verifiable lab licensing revenue.

**Labs buying direct — does it undercut brokers?** Labs negotiate direct at the marquee tier (OpenAI ~20 publisher deals; Google-Reddit ~$60M/yr; Meta-News Corp up to $50M/yr; Amazon-NYT >$20M/yr; Anthropic zero publisher deals but a $1.5B book settlement). But mid-market operational data needs rights review, de-identification, packaging, and aggregation before labs can use it, and labs are outsourcing exactly that. The broker wedge is strongest in SMB/mid-market operational data. Structural shift: 2024 was one-time training dumps; 2026 is moving toward recurring retrieval/citation licensing, which rewards ongoing broker relationships.

## 3. Pewter Data + Competitors Teardown

### Pewter Data (pewterdata.com) — the positioning comp
- **Kicker:** "For owners of US firms with 20+ employees"
- **Hero:** "Your old email and files are worth six figures to AI labs. We handle the entire sale."
- **Subhead:** "Labs are paying established firms to license de-identified copies of records they already keep. A 60-person accounting firm was paid $350,000. A 100-person freight broker, $650,000. Pewter runs your deal from valuation to wire. You take one call and approve the terms."
- **Trust strip:** "No files to start. No cost to you. You keep your originals."
- **Funnel:** (1) hero with interactive payout estimator (employees + years in business → dollar range via "Get my offer"); (2) proof stack — deal cards ($650K freight broker, $450K engineering, $350K accounting), market table ($1B buyer commitment, $10M Spirit Airlines bid), Forbes CEO quote, "Fifteen firms paid in 2026" list ($110K-$1.2M) with an honesty footnote that figures are unverified publisher statements; (3) education — "The labs have read the internet. They have never seen how your business runs"; (4) before/after de-id demo email ("the work survives and the people disappear"); (5) qualification (20+ people, 3+ years records, US) + 8-question FAQ; (6) 4-step close: request offer (2 min) → confidential call (1 business day) → written offer → de-identify, deliver, get paid. Owner effort framed as ~1 hour.
- **Best FAQ answers:** "Is this real money, or a pitch?" → "Real. Our licensing partner has paid $350,000 to $650,000 to firms of 60 to 100 people." / "What does this cost me?" → "Nothing, at any stage. Our fee is paid by our licensing partner when a deal closes. If there's no deal, there's no fee." / "Do I lose my data?" → "No. The deal is a license of an agreed, de-identified copy. Your original files and your ownership of them don't change."
- **Partner/referral program:** exists (10% of the business's payout, $100K cap, 12-month first-to-register credit). Corrected Oct 13; the earlier "none found" was wrong.
- **Red flag:** the paying "licensing partner" is never named; no team, funding, or lab relationships verifiable.

### Troveo (troveo.ai) — direct rival, pivoted into company data
- "Real-World Data Powering Top Frontier Labs." Company-data card: "License Your Data — Turn your company's email, chat, CRM, tickets, and files into recurring revenue."
- Trust: $20M+ paid to rights holders; 40+ active buyers; "we take no fees and no deductions from your payouts"; 7,000+ providers, 150 countries; named partners (Barstool Sports, 100 Thieves).
- Funnel: free 10-question data value assessment → 6-step process ("the first sale is the floor, and you're paid on every sale after it"). Partner deck exists with a contact email; commission splits not published.

### Defined.ai (defined.ai/partnership-programs) — closest to Jay's partner-led model
- "Turn Your Existing Data Into Recurring Revenue." Two tracks: Data Partners (licensing fees from marketplace sales) and AI Service Suppliers.
- Trust: "319% YoY growth in dataset sales"; "$600K+ Average partner revenue"; "$1M+ Revenue for 5+ partners"; "100+ partners"; GDPR + ISO 27001/27701/42001 badges; Gartner citation.
- Funnel: 4-step onboarding (Jira intake → market research → data eval → ethics/compliance). Revenue split % not published.

### TollBit (tollbit.com) — publisher AI-bot licensing
- "Future-proof your content for any AI protocol with TollBit. Control access, analyze traffic, and monetize as the agent economy grows."
- Trust: client logos (Reuters, AP, TIME, Forbes, Bloomberg, CNN); 987B+ visits analyzed, 22B+ AI scrapes detected; publishers keep 100% of set rate, TollBit fees the AI buyer; 2,000+ clients, 500 publishers.

### Dappier (dappier.com) — publisher AI-data marketplace (homepage pivoted Oct 2026 to agent storefront)
- Prior: "the world's first online marketplace for AI content and data rights"; pay-per-query; publishers set terms/pricing. Named partners: Benzinga, News-Press & Gazette, LiveRamp. $2M seed.

### Human Native AI — acquired by Cloudflare (Jan 2026)
- Rights-holder marketplace; £2.8M seed; took a cut per deal. Now folded into Cloudflare. Relevant as validation and a removed competitor.

## 5. Learn-From Shortlist (sign under to learn the ropes)

Tradeoff: signing under someone teaches you deal mechanics, buyer expectations, and de-id standards fast, but you earn capped economics and build their pipeline, not yours. Going direct keeps full economics but you learn by doing.

1. **Defined.ai Data Partners track** — Best classroom: the most mature partner onboarding in the space (Jira intake, data eval, compliance checks). Learn their qualification and packaging standards, then apply them to your own direct deals.
2. **Troveo's partner program** — Learn licensor-side mechanics from a marketplace that pays on every sale ("the first sale is the floor"). Their 6-step process is a ready-made SOP template.
3. **Pewter Data (as an observer, not a partner)** — A partner program exists (10% of the business's payout, $100k cap per business, 12-month first-to-register credit), but the payer is anonymous. Their site is the best-executed conversion funnel in the lane. Study the estimator, proof stack, FAQ, and risk-reversal language; do not route deals through an unnamed payer.
4. **A working data broker practitioner** — No verified individual names surfaced in this sweep. Do not sign under anyone you cannot verify has real lab payouts.

## 6. Troveo Resource Summary

Page: https://www.troveo.ai/resources/sell-data-to-ai-companies ("How to Sell Data to AI Companies," Aug 2026). This is the category-education playbook for Jay's exact business.

1. **Opportunity framing:** "If you searched 'how to sell data to AI companies,' you're ahead of most businesses." Labs signed deals from tens of thousands to a quarter billion; buying moved past famous publishers into creator catalogs, call recordings, gameplay, support tickets, even a defunct airline's archive.
2. **What sells:** real-world video, voice/audio, gameplay, robotics video, content catalogs, and the overlooked newest category — business/operational data: support tickets, CRM histories, project records, transaction logs ("to you it looks like exhaust").
3. **Price anchors:** News Corp ~$250M/5yr to OpenAI; Google ~$60M/yr to Reddit; Shutterstock $104M in one year; Spirit Airlines $10M Google bid. Value levers: scarcity, exclusivity, clean rights, training readiness.
4. **License, don't sell:** "In almost every case you don't sell your data, you license it." Keep ownership, defined rights/period/purpose; exclusive commands a premium; non-exclusive earns repeatedly ("the first sale is the floor"). Privacy/rights work before anything ships.
5. **Their funnel:** free 5-minute "data value assessment" (10 questions); $20M+ paid to 7,000+ holders; 95% exclusive; company never named publicly; paid on every sale. FAQ: "Typical full-company deals start at six figures."

## 8. Red Flags

1. **Micro1 split-vs-bounty mismatch.** Public program = flat bounties (up to $50K), no revenue split. Jay's split deal must be verified in the agreement text before partners are recruited on split economics.
2. **Pewter's anonymous payer.** "Our licensing partner" is never named; no team, funding, or lab relationships verifiable. Treat as unvetted broker. Do not route deals through it until the payer is named and a payout independently confirmed.
3. **Pewter's claimed deals are self-reported.** The 15 "firms paid in 2026" ($110K-$1.2M) are publisher statements; Pewter itself says it did not advise on the third-party ones and did not verify.
4. **Micro1's $1B is 4 days old and financed, not funded.** Citi/Hercules capital "provided"; actual deployed dollars unknown.
5. **Micro1's $4B valuation and >$500M run rate are company-reported via press, unaudited.** Forbes sources; company declined comment.
6. **Spirit Airlines bid controversy.** Privacy ombudsman wanted more vetting time; unions/lawmakers objected; micro1 proposed self-de-identification. Hearing Oct 14, 2026. Aggressive posture on contested data.
7. **No competitor publishes partner commission splits.** Anyone quoting splits without a signed agreement is guessing.
8. **Vana token down ~82%; consumer-lane players (Kled, Verb) have no verifiable lab revenue.** Not relevant to company-data deals.
9. **"Verma Media" does not exist** as an AI-data company after multiple searches. Do not reference it.
10. **Labs buy direct at the marquee tier.** The broker wedge is SMB/mid-market operational data, not News Corp-scale deals. Don't pitch partners on top-tier economics.
