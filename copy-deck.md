## Voice principles
- One reader: a 55-year-old owner of a 60-person logistics firm who has never
  heard of data licensing. Every sentence must survive her.
- Short sentences. Concrete nouns. Numbers beat adjectives.
- Say "license," never "sell." Say "you keep ownership" every time it matters.
- Lead with the money, then the safety, then the simplicity. In that order.
- Honest caveats are a feature. "An illustration only" converts skeptics.
- We, never I. No em dashes. No hype adjectives.

### How it works
**Head:** You take one call. We do everything else.
**Body:** [COMPANY] is your single point of contact from the first estimate to
the day the payment lands. No shopping your data around, no IT project, no one
else calling you.

**What you do (about 1 hour):**
1. Answer a few questions. Two minutes, below. No files, no logins.
2. Take one confidential call. About 30 minutes, at a time you choose.
3. Approve the offer. Read the terms and sign if you like them.

**What we handle:**
1. Valuation. We size your records by system, years, and sector, and tell you
   what they are realistically worth.
2. The offer. We prepare it with our licensing buyer and bring it to you in
   writing: price, scope, permitted use, payment schedule.
3. Negotiation. We work through price, exclusivity, and scope before anything
   reaches your desk.
4. Export and de-identification. We coordinate the export with your IT person
   or provider. Names, contact details, account numbers, and credentials are
   removed before anything is delivered.
5. Closing and payment. We track every milestone to the wire and stay your
   contact afterward.

**Risk reversal:** No offer, no cost. There is no fee to the owner at any
stage. If no offer is made, or you do not like the one we bring, you walk away
and nothing has left your systems. You approve every term in writing before a
single record is exported.

### The process (four steps)
1. Today, 2 minutes. **Request your offer.** Headcount, years in business, and
   where your records live. Nothing uploaded.
2. Within 1 business day. **Confidential call.** We confirm what you have and
   give you a realistic range before any review.
3. After data review. **Written offer.** Price, scope, permitted use, and
   payment milestones, in one agreement you can read in an evening.
4. On delivery. **De-identify, deliver, get paid.** We coordinate the agreed

## FAQ BANK

**Is this real money, or a pitch?**
Real. Our licensing buyer's published tiers run $100K+ for qualified firms,
$500K+ at scale, and $1M+ for highly unique data. Google's $10M winning bid
for an airline's records is in a federal court filing. Your offer comes in
writing before you commit to anything.

**What does this cost me?**
Nothing, at any stage. Our fee is paid by the licensing buyer when a deal
closes. If there's no deal, there's no fee.

**Do I lose my data?**
No. The deal is a license of an agreed, de-identified copy. Your original
files and your ownership of them don't change.

**Will my clients or employees be exposed?**
Names, contact details, account numbers, credentials, and payment details are
removed or replaced before delivery. Regulated, privileged, or third-party
records can be excluded. No method removes every risk, so you review the
process and a sample before you sign.

**Who will use the data, and for what?**
Authorized AI developers, for training and evaluating models, as defined in
your agreement. Exclusivity and permitted uses are written into the terms you
approve.

**How much of my team's time does it take?**
About an hour of yours. If an export is needed, we coordinate it with your IT
person or provider. Nobody creates new data or cleans anything up.

**What if I don't know what data we have?**
Most owners don't. Tell us which tools you've used over the years (email,
shared drives, your accounting or operating system) and we work out what's
there on the call.

**Why act now?**
Labs are buying now. And the value lives in history you still have: when
subscriptions lapse or a firm winds down, archives get deleted and that value
is gone.
